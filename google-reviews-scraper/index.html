Google Reviews Scraper: Ratings, Text & Owner Replies

Google reviews scraper, API and MCP tool for any business: stars, text, date, translation, photos, owner reply and the business's rating. Paste Google Maps links, place IDs, CIDs or "business name, city". No reviewer names. Only-new-reviews mode. Pay per review plus small fetch and start fees.

Try it on Apify  

Typical uses

  • Reputation monitoring: turn on Only new reviews, schedule a daily run and send new 1- and 2-star Google reviews to Slack or a spreadsheet: you never get (or pay for) the same review twice.
  • Owner reply audits: see which reviews a business answered, when, and what it said (`ownerAnswer`, `ownerAnswerAt`).
  • Agencies and local SEO: compare review volume, rating and reply rate of your clients and their competitors.
  • Sentiment analysis and AI summaries: feed clean review text (translated into one language, original kept) to an LLM, a BI tool or an AI agent (MCP).
  • Market research: what customers praise or complain about across the hotels, restaurants or shops of a city.

Ready-to-run examples

  • Export a business's Google reviews with the owner's replies: Export the newest Google reviews of a business (The Plaza hotel, New York here) with stars, text, translation, photos, topic ratings and the owner's reply and its date. Change the business to any name and city.

Run it from your code

Get an API token in the Apify Console (Settings → Integrations), then:

curl -X POST \
  "https://api.apify.com/v2/acts/plainrows~google-reviews-scraper/run-sync-get-dataset-items?token=$APIFY_TOKEN" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{
  "places": [
    "The Plaza Hotel, New York"
  ],
  "maxReviewsPerPlace": 100,
  "country": "US"
}'
from apify_client import ApifyClient

client = ApifyClient("<YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN>")
run = client.actor("plainrows/google-reviews-scraper").call(run_input={'places': ['The Plaza Hotel, New York'],
 'maxReviewsPerPlace': 100,
 'country': 'US'})
for row in client.dataset(run["defaultDatasetId"]).iterate_items():
    print(row)
