After pytrends: Google Trends data in your code, options compared

You want Google Trends data inside a script, a spreadsheet, n8n or an AI agent: interest over time, interest by region, related queries and topics. Here is where the usual options stand, and what PlainRows offers.

The options and their limits

  • Official Google Trends API: on 2026-10-08 it was still an alpha. Google writes that it is "accepting applications for alpha testers" and is "prioritizing developers that know what they want to do"; the data is a rolling window of the last 5 years (developers.google.com/search/apis/trends).
  • pytrends: the popular Python library was archived by its owner on April 17, 2025 and is read-only. Its README says "This is not an official or supported API" and that the rate limit "is not publicly known" (github.com/GeneralMills/pytrends).
  • SerpApi Google Trends API: each request returns one data type (interest over time, by region, related topics or related queries), and related queries, related topics and interest by region accept a single keyword per search (serpapi.com/google-trends-api). Plans start at $25 for 1,000 searches a month, with a free plan of 250 searches (serpapi.com/pricing). The full picture of one keyword therefore takes four searches.

When another option is the better choice

  • You are accepted into the official Google Trends API alpha: consistently scaled data straight from Google.
  • You already pay for a SerpApi plan with unused searches, or you only need a few searches a month (SerpApi's free plan).
  • You need an answer in under a few seconds on every call. In Standard speed a PlainRows search waits in the data provider's queue, usually about a minute; Fast speed answers in about 10-30 seconds for an extra $0.01 per search.

When PlainRows is the better choice

  • You want interest over time, regions and related queries for a keyword in one call and one price.
  • You run it from n8n, Make, Zapier, Google Sheets or an AI agent (MCP) and want flat rows, not nested chart JSON.
  • You want a hard cap: every run stops at the maximum cost you set.
